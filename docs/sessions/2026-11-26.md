## Concierge speaks, listens, can be interrupted, wakes on its name

- **Spoken alerts** (`9480f46`). Alerts marked `notify` are said aloud with the
  Mac's own voices (`Speaker.swift`, `Concierge --say <voice id> <text>`).
  Quiet hours (08:30 to 21:00), quiet during calendar events (from
  `briefings/agenda/<day>.json`), silence for an hour or until tomorrow from
  a toolbar speaker or Settings. The pure rules live in `tick.py`
  (`may_speak`, `spoken`) with Python tests in `Tests/python`, run by
  `Scripts/test.sh`. The default Apple voice sounded robotic; the owner
  downloaded **Jamie (Premium)**, which "Best English voice" now picks.
- **Talk-back** (`90edf6d`). A microphone button (Cmd-T) starts a spoken
  conversation: on-device speech recognition, the question box answers with a
  note to reply in one to three spoken sentences, Jamie says it, it listens
  again until a pause, "thanks" or 8 seconds of quiet. When the window is
  open, `--say` hands the alert to the running app over a distributed
  notification, and the app listens 6 seconds for a reply ("Anything I can
  do?"). Typed and spoken questions share `AppModel.ask(_:prompt:)`.
- **Interrupting** (`addd15c`, `4a5ea91`). `VoiceIO.swift` plays speech
  through the same AVAudioEngine as the microphone with voice processing on,
  so echo cancellation removes Concierge's own voice. Words mostly not in the
  sentence being said, or "stop"/"wait", cut it off and become the owner's
  turn (`VoiceIO.isBargeIn`). Two bugs on the way: the engine failed with
  avfaudio -10875 until the output node existed before voice processing was
  switched on, and `AVSpeechSynthesizer.write` signals its end twice, which
  crashed the app until the continuation resumed only once.
- **Wake word** (`c4a4810`, `2f36462`). `WakeListener.swift` listens for
  "Concierge" whenever the app runs, on-device only, paused while silenced or
  during a conversation; Settings switch. Words after it are the first
  question; the word alone gets "Yes?". The first version acted 1.2 seconds
  after the wake word and sent "Watson" for "what's on"; it now waits 1.5 to
  2 seconds.

All checked by the owner by voice. 42 Swift tests and 10 Python tests pass.
CPU while waiting for the wake word: about 0.6%.
