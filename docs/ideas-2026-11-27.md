# Ideas to improve Nestor (review of 2026-11-27)

From reading the code, the skills, the run log (45 runs, 24 to 27 November)
and three web searches. **Built the same day: ideas 1, 2, 12, 13 and 14,
and the microphone part of idea 5.** The rest is open. Where an idea says
"pick N", the number is from the list as it stood that morning.

## What the run log shows

| Fact | Number |
| --- | --- |
| Tokens sent with each question | 135,000 to 195,000 |
| Wait for the first question after a page build | 10 to 14 s |
| Wait for later questions | 2 to 7 s |
| Cost of a question after a 5 minute gap | $0.13 to $0.20 |
| Page build | 58 turns, about 2 minutes, about $1 |
| Daytime checks | 20 runs, $0.44 in all |
| Pause that ends a spoken turn | 1.6 s |

A question continues the newest conversation in the folder. After a page
build that is the build itself, so every question carries the whole build.

## A. Faster, steadier voice

1. **Give questions their own conversation.** Start it from the page's text,
   today's updates and the agenda, not from the build. Use `--session-id` so
   the app picks the conversation. Small. Biggest single cut in wait and cost.
2. **Speak the answer as it arrives.** `claude -p --output-format stream-json
   --verbose --include-partial-messages` gives the text in pieces. Cut at
   sentence ends and say each one. Today the whole answer is waited for, then
   turned into sound in one go. Small to medium.
3. **Keep one process open for a conversation.** `--input-format stream-json`
   takes several questions on one process. The exact message shape is only
   documented through the Agent SDK, so test it first. Medium.
4. **Answer simple things with no model.** "What's next", "what's on
   tomorrow", "anything urgent", the time. Read them from the agenda file
   and the page. Needs idea 11 to be solid. Medium.
5. **Listen on the Mac's own microphone, and skip echo cancelling on
   headphones.** Extends pick 5. The headset then never drops to call
   quality, and the quarter-core cost goes away on headphones, where there is
   little echo. Built-in microphone with Bluetooth output fails with echo
   cancelling on (error -10875), so the two go together. Small to medium.
6. **A better end of turn.** Silero VAD plus Smart Turn v3 (8 MB, 23
   languages with French, BSD) decides from the sound whether the speaker is
   done. The 1.6 s pause could drop to about 0.3 s. Medium. No French
   accuracy figure found.
7. **One recogniser for both languages.** Parakeet v3 through FluidAudio
   detects the language itself: French 5.9% and English 5.4% word errors by
   its maker's figures. No more "speak French". Apple's newer engine
   (SpeechAnalyzer) is the no-dependency option, 2.1% against 9.0% word
   errors in one English test, but it may hear the name worse. Medium.
8. **A wake word model made for the job.** livekit-wakeword: Apache, Swift
   package, 7 MB, a custom name trained in about an hour. Same CPU as today,
   so the gain is fewer misses, not speed. Training needs a LiveKit Cloud
   project. Medium.
9. **Keep the last two seconds of sound.** A guess at the unexplained
   "nothing heard": the switch of echo cancelling at the start may drop
   words. A short rolling buffer handed to the recogniser would cover it.
   Untested.
10. **A key to talk.** A system-wide shortcut held down while speaking.
    Works when the wake word does not. Small.

## B. A morning page that cannot fail quietly

11. **The model returns facts, the app draws the page.** Each section comes
    back as checked data (`--json-schema`), and the app fills the layout.
    Keys, escaping and links are then right by construction. It also opens
    the door to a phone view, search across days, the voice walk-through and
    scoring. A stopped section loses one section, as now. Large. The change
    most other ideas lean on.
12. **Build the morning page from Nestor's own scheduler.** Today a Claude
    desktop task does it. That run is not in the cost log, has no retry and
    no catch-up after sleep. The Mac was asleep at 07:20 on 27 November. The
    daytime checks already prove a launchd run reaches Gmail and WhatsApp.
    Then send the phone notice when the page is ready (pick 12). Small.
13. **Check the page before showing it.** Links only to known sites, no
    remote pictures, every item has a key and a source link. The page view
    blocks nothing today: a picture address in a mail summary would be
    fetched. Another mail assistant leaked mail exactly that way. Small.
14. **Warn before the sign-in ends.** The connectors need the `/login`
    sign-in, which has an end date. `claude auth status` shows it. Tell the
    owner ten days ahead, and say on the page when a source was down two
    days running. Small.
15. **CI from GitHub itself, not from mail.** A script asks `gh` for the
    last runs of each repo before the model starts. Exact "red since" ages,
    no tokens. Small.
16. **One reader per source, then one writer.** Helpers on a cheap model
    read mail, calendar and chats side by side. The main model only writes.
    Not known whether helpers can reach the connectors. Medium.

## C. Memory: promises and people

17. **A list of promises, both ways.** "I owe" and "waiting on", taken from
    sent mail and chats. After some weekdays with no reply, a nudge with a
    ready follow-up draft. This is the feature people keep in the write-ups
    read. Risk: promises that were never made. Each one must link to its
    source. Medium.
18. **Notes on people.** One short file each: who they are, language, tone,
    last exchange. Feeds meeting prep and drafts. Keep them under
    `briefings/`, so Keymodule's Slack stays where the rule puts it. Medium.
19. **Kinds of thread that get a flag, not a draft.** Money, legal, hard
    conversations. And a tone per person for the rest. Small.
20. **A weekly page.** What stayed open, what was put off three times, the
    week ahead. Small once idea 17 exists.

## D. During the day

21. **A new WhatsApp message starts a check at once.** The chat database is
    local, so a script can see a change for free. A message from Emma then
    waits minutes, not up to two hours. Mail stays on the clock. Medium.
22. **Slack in the daytime check.** It is read only in the morning now. Small.
23. **Watches.** "Tell me when Declan replies", "when CI is green". A short
    list the check reads. Cap it at about ten. Small to medium.
24. **Reminders and notes by voice.** "Remind me at four to call…" is kept
    by the scheduler with no model. "Note that…" goes to the Obsidian inbox.
    Small.
25. **Walk through the page by voice.** "Nestor, brief me" reads the urgent
    items one at a time. "Next", "done", "later", "draft a reply" use the
    buttons that exist. Medium, easier after idea 11.
26. **A plan for the day.** Travel time before events with a place, a
    warning on a crowded day, web lookup before a meeting with someone new.
    A proposal only, never a calendar change. Medium.

## E. Knowing whether it is good

27. **More than "×".** "Not urgent", "wrong" and "more like this" on each
    item, and a visible list of what Nestor learned that can be deleted.
    Small.
28. **A score from data already kept.** Share of urgent items handled
    against dismissed, and urgent things found late. Save one day's inputs
    and replay it to compare models. The Sonnet trial due 2026-12-03 is
    judged by eye today. Medium.

## Suggested order

1, 2, 12, 13, 14 first: all small, all fix something seen in the log or the
code. Then 17 and 11.

## Not verified

- The message shape for a process kept open (idea 3).
- Whether helpers reach the connectors (idea 16).
- French accuracy of Smart Turn and of the newer Apple engine.
- The cause of "nothing heard" (idea 9 is a guess).
- Whether the 07:20 run happened on 27 November: the page was rebuilt at
  11:47, which hides it.
- `--bare` would be faster but drops skills, connectors and the
  subscription sign-in, so it does not fit.
